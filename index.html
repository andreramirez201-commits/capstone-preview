Business Problem

Ask yourselves: What's actually wrong today? Describe the situation in plain language, no jargon.

Example: "Proposal drafting takes about 14 hours, win rates have dropped, and leads wait 5+ days for follow-up."

If this was auto-filled from your uploaded document, read it over — it's often the most important sentence in your whole preview, since it becomes your slide's headline.

Ask yourselves: What slows the process down day to day? What causes errors, rework, or complaints? What's the biggest bottleneck?

Example: "Reps rebuild proposals from scratch for every client." / "Follow-up falls through the cracks after the first message." / "Manual handoffs delay approvals by days."

Aim for 2-4 challenges. Be specific — "things are slow" is less useful than "proposals take 14 hours to draft."

Ask yourselves: What number shows how big this problem is today? How long does it take? How often does it fail? What does it cost?

Example: "Proposal drafting: ~14 hours." / "Follow-up delay: 5+ days for some leads."

Use real numbers from the brief if you have them. If not, make a reasonable estimate and say so.

Ask yourselves: Before any AI, what are the actual steps today, in order? Where does it get stuck or slow down?

Example: "Sales Conversation -> Manual Notes -> Manual Proposal Drafting -> Review -> Proposal Sent -> Manual Follow-Up"

Separate each step with an arrow (->). Fill this in alongside Proposed Workflow below for a real before/after process comparison, not just a before/after number.

Ask yourselves: What are the steps, in order, from raw input to final output? Where does a human check the work?

Example: "Sales Notes -> Opportunity Brief -> AI Draft -> Quality Check -> Human Approval -> AI-Assisted Follow-Up"

Separate each step with an arrow (->) — it turns into connected boxes automatically.

Ask yourselves: What changed, in numbers? Pick one or two metrics you can measure both before and after.

Example: "Before: 14 hours to draft a proposal" / "After: 20 minutes with AI assistance"

If Step 1's document upload found numbers in your problem statement, the Before: lines above are already filled in for you — type your team's real, measured result right after each "After:" once you have it.

Keep the units consistent between Before and After (both in hours, or both in minutes) — mixed units can't be charted fairly, and the preview will fall back to a plain list.

Responsible AI & Human Review

Ask yourselves: What could go wrong if this AI solution is used carelessly, and what is your team doing to prevent it?

Example: "Accuracy: verify AI-generated claims before they go out" / "Overpromising: human review of outcomes, pricing, scope" / "Confidentiality: no client PII in public AI tools" / "Transparency: internal usage policy" / "Human approval: nothing sent without salesperson sign-off"

Cover accuracy, overpromising, confidentiality, transparency, and human approval — your rubric likely asks for all five.

Ask yourselves: At what exact point does a person check the AI's work? What do they check for, and what happens if they reject it?

Example: "Every AI-drafted proposal and follow-up is reviewed by the salesperson for accuracy, tone, and pricing before it's sent. Nothing goes to a client without that approval. AI Drafts. Humans Decide."

Testing

Ask yourselves: Before you can say a prompt "worked," what did you actually judge it against?

Example: "Accuracy: did it stay within the info we gave it" / "Personalization: did it reflect the specific client" / "Clarity" / "Usefulness" / "Efficiency: did it reduce meaningful manual drafting" / "Human editing required: how much rewriting before it was usable"

This is your rubric, stated once — the next field is what you found when you applied it.

Ask yourselves: What did you actually test your prompts against? Where did a first attempt produce a weak or wrong output, and what did you change to fix it?

Example: "Tested all 5 prompts against a consistent fictional Meridian opportunity. Prompt 2's first draft invented a delivery date we never provided — added an explicit 'do not invent dates' instruction and retested."

A polished final output with no record of testing doesn't show iteration — that's specifically what this field is for.

Measurement

Ask yourselves: How will you know this actually worked? Pick measures the client already tracks, or could easily start tracking.

Example: "Proposal drafting time" / "Lead follow-up time" / "Proposal win rate"

Pick 3 — that's what your cohort's rubric asks for.

Implementation Plan

Ask yourselves: Who's in the pilot, how long does it run, and what baseline are you measuring against?

Example: "Pilot with 2-3 reps for 30 days. Track drafting time and follow-up time against the 14-hour/5-day baseline."

Ask yourselves: What do pilot participants need to know before day one — the workflow, the tool, the responsible-AI rules?

Example: "Train participants on the AI-assisted workflow and responsible-AI rules before the pilot starts."

Ask yourselves: During the actual rollout, at what points does a human have to step in and approve, override, or take over?

Example: "Every AI output still requires human approval before it reaches a client — this doesn't change during the pilot."

Ask yourselves: Even if the tool works, what could make reps not actually use it? Distrust of AI output, extra steps, unclear ownership?

Example: "Reps may distrust AI-drafted proposals at first — mitigate by keeping human approval mandatory and sharing early wins from the pilot group."

Ask yourselves: How does the pilot group tell you what's working and what isn't, while it's still happening?

Example: "Collect weekly feedback from the pilot group on output quality and how much editing was needed."

Ask yourselves: After the pilot runs, what do you actually look at — drafting time, follow-up time, output quality, human editing, feedback? What do you change based on that?

Example: "Compare drafting/follow-up times against baseline. Review pilot feedback and human-editing patterns. Refine prompts and the workflow based on what failed most often."

Ask yourselves: What has to be true for you to recommend scaling this beyond the pilot? What would make you NOT recommend it yet?

Example: "Recommend scaling if drafting time and follow-up time both improved without a drop in proposal quality or win rate, and the pilot group actually adopted the workflow. Otherwise, extend the pilot and keep iterating."

Ask yourselves: If the client said yes today, what's the one-line bottom-line recommendation the team is making?

Example: "Meridian should pilot the AI-assisted workflow before broader implementation." / "Do not present untested targets as results."

Your first item becomes this slide's headline if the phases above are empty, so lead with the most concrete action.
